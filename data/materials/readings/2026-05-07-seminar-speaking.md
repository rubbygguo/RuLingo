# Speaking Up in Seminars

Many students understand the reading before a seminar but still find it difficult to speak during discussion. This is especially common when the discussion moves quickly or when other students seem more confident. However, seminar participation does not always require a perfect answer. In many cases, a clear question, a brief summary, or a thoughtful connection to another idea can also make a valuable contribution.

One practical way to prepare is to write down three types of sentences before class. The first is a summary sentence, such as "The author seems to argue that..." The second is a response sentence, such as "I agree with this point to some extent because..." The third is a question sentence, such as "Could we also consider this from the perspective of...?" These sentence frames reduce pressure because they give students a starting point.

Another useful strategy is to build on other people's comments. Instead of introducing a completely new idea, students can say, "Building on what you just said..." or "That relates to the point about..." This makes participation feel more natural and less like a performance.

Over time, speaking in seminars becomes easier when students treat it as a regular habit rather than a test of intelligence. The goal is not to sound perfect, but to practise thinking in English with other people.
